The Final Push — Jul 21–22 Context

At 01:31 AM ET July 22, after 14+ hours on July 21 alone and 115+ cumulative hours, Jeff Trbovich wrote the most important product insight in the entire session log:

"Having an AI orchestration layer that seeks to take the place of a human out of the loop along with their instinct, years of deep customer knowledge that isn't written ANYWHERE in ISC or in the hallowed halls of IBM — it's in the heads of the humans who worked on those accounts. Duey and every other sales exec cannot trust AI to make those calls. It's easier for a plane to fly and land itself than it is to do this because of the incomplete, stale, and/or human practice of loading opps into ISC that's not based in fact nor instinct in order to meet some pipeline/forecast metric."

This is the correct design thesis. The app was built exactly right: AI handles the mechanical preparation. The human makes the judgment calls. That's not a limitation — it's the product.

Why This Couldn't Be Done Another Way

The user's core assertion — that iterative AI-assisted development delivered more in 12 days than a traditional spec-then-build approach could — is well-supported by the log:

  • Session 1 alone required 8 distinct authentication architecture attempts before finding the HAR solution. A traditional approach would have stalled here for days waiting for Salesforce OAuth approval.
  • 5 consecutive sessions (32–36) were spent on a single CSS bug (border-collapse: collapse silently killing position: sticky). Each session built on the previous diagnosis. No spec document could have anticipated this.
  • The app's architecture changed 3 times in 12 days: rule-based → watsonx-augmented → multi-user SSO → Pipeline Intelligence Engine → Column Picker PPT. Each pivot happened because the prior version was working and visible, triggering the next insight.
  • 52 sessions = 52 closed feedback loops. A traditional waterfall spec → design → build → test cycle for the same feature set would take 6–8 weeks minimum with a 2-person team.
  • The AI reviewer battle (4 rounds, Session 52) is itself a perfect demonstration of the methodology: problem → diagnosis → fix → resubmit → repeat until green. That's exactly how every feature in this app was built.
